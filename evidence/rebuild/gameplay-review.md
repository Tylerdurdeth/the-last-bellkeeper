# First-area gameplay and story assessment — 16 September 2026

Scope: current UI/captions, state flow in `game/main.js`, layout/landmarks, and the actual full desktop/touch routes already exercised in the runtime reports. No new performance test, asset changes, spending or agents. I already know the route; this is **not a blind discovery playtest**. Earlier canopy/terrain screenshots reflect that captured revision; the lead is independently repairing current visibility. This review does not review or approve my own movement/animation aesthetic.

## GAMEPLAY verdict — meaningful improvement, still below the requested discovery bar

The original criticism is partly addressed. The objective no longer exposes both pickup and destination on half a screen; there is no permanent target arrow. The camera follows the player around bends. Running and jumping work, one optional keepsake requires getting onto a rock, the bridge changes traversal, and the completed area remains explorable. Context-sensitive action labels are appropriate input affordances and should stay.

However, the main interaction chain is still approach chimes → press action → approach nearby gust → press action → retrace route → press action at wheel → cross and press action. There is no essential judgment beyond finding the interaction volumes. Ringing the chimes and catching the wind happen a few metres apart; the chime is currently another activation button, not an opportunity to infer anything. Carrying the current changes the visible held ribbon and HUD, but not movement decisions or the return route. The tested main route never needed a jump. Optional memories add detours, but two are effectively glowing pickups on open ground.

Observed speed-run evidence is 115.5m/35.5 seconds for the known main route and 130.24m/48.69 seconds for a known full touch route including all three memories. Those measurements are not first-time play duration, but they expose how much of the experience is currently travel. A longer map or slower walking would not make the decisions richer. I would not yet claim the first area provides the Zelda-like exploration/discovery requested by the user.

## STORY verdict — coherent tone, insufficient personal cause and consequence

The language has a consistent gentle voice: a missing keeper, fresh ribbons, copper feather, an inscription about future mornings, a ribbon toward home. The acts of listening and restoring a crossing fit the bellkeeper identity. The cottage suggests someone has a life here, rather than a generic puzzle dispenser.

But the scene has atmosphere instead of a small dramatic arc. The player does not learn why this apprentice cares about this crossing, who used the keepsakes, or what their absence means. The three descriptions are interchangeable mood fragments: finding one changes no later recognition or reaction. The final “one bell answers another” line states a payoff without establishing a particular other bell or person the player has learned to anticipate. The story remains understandable at task level, but not memorable at character level. This is a bounded first-area gap; it does not require a cutscene, dialogue tree or exposition dump.

## Three concrete improvements, in priority order

1. **Make the cottage clue and final answer refer to the same absent person.** Add one optional inspectable trace at the existing porch: two bell-tool pegs, one empty, with the keeper's short note about the far-bank morning bell. The apprentice can respond in one specific personal line, establishing a relationship without explaining the whole plot. Reuse the existing feather/inscription/ribbon as evidence of that keeper's daily route; change the final acknowledgement if a memory was found, so observation earns recognition. Keep them optional and individually readable; no “collect all three to proceed” gate. Reveal the keepsake counter after the first discovery if the intended tone is curiosity rather than a checklist. Success criterion: a player can say who they think used this place and why answering the far bell matters, using something they observed.

2. **Turn chime → capture into one readable act of interpretation.** Ringing the existing chime should make the current reveal itself by moving leaves/flowers in a short sequence through the existing garden, with the visible ribbon briefly passing behind the root/fern cluster. Give the player two nearby pockets they can inspect, one sheltered from the breeze and one responsive; the correct pocket visibly reacts when the bell is raised. Keep capture forgiving and available whenever the player approaches/intercepts the visible current. Do not add a forced wait cycle, precision rhythm test or arbitrary wrong-answer penalty. The action label can still say Catch once close, but the world should have explained where and why to approach first. Success criterion: the player follows a physical response and makes a small inference, rather than simply moving from one proximity-enabled button to the next.

3. **Give carrying wind a useful route choice on the way home.** Within the current map, connect an existing return bend back toward the cottage/wheel via a short root ledge crossing (roughly one safe jump). While charged, nearby flowers/bells answer and reveal that shortcut; the long familiar path remains fully usable. The shortcut should overlook a place already visited so the player understands how the woods connect. Missing the jump returns the player safely without deleting the collected wind or repeating the outward trip. It can pass one of the current optional keepsakes, giving exploration a tangible payoff without extra collectibles or new territory. Success criterion: carrying changes what the player notices and chooses; the return is recognition plus mastery rather than identical backtracking.

These changes should be tested on the same scene before adding another region. A known-route repeat can remain quick. Approval needs an unbriefed human to find the garden, explain the chime response, recognize the return shortcut and describe the small story; the existing automated route passes cannot answer those questions.
